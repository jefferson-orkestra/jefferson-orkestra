### What I do

I build the operational software a business opens every morning: dispatching a field team, the app the worker actually uses, and the money that has to reconcile at the end of the month — the part most operational software usually never reaches.

Not "AI everywhere". The right tool for each layer: deterministic pipelines where rules suffice, a model only where interpretation is needed. Every case study below says plainly what the system does on its own, what it hands back to a person, and what it measurably replaced.

### In production

**[Live Portugal — the system that runs the day](https://github.com/jefferson-orkestra/portfolio/blob/main/case-studies/live-portugal-ai-os.md)** · in production every day since May 2026

A Lisbon tour operator planned its week on a printed grid taped to a wall and typed customer reminders one at a time in six languages. Today the operator photographs that grid and it becomes a real schedule; 15 drivers are dispatched from it and see only their own work on their phone; every driver's balance settles as the day closes; and customer messages write themselves in the right language at the right time. Sending is one tap, deliberately — an automated send from the company's main number would risk the channel the business runs on.

`Next.js 15` · `TypeScript` · `Supabase` · `n8n` · `Claude API` · `WhatsApp Business` · `Google Calendar`

**Measured:** 6 hours of manual work removed per week · first customer response down from about 2 hours to under 1 minute · 279 of 281 messages delivered (99.3%) across 6 languages · 6,183 bookings synced · 83 driver dispatches across 76 tours.

> **Full disclosure:** I work at that company. I built the system that runs the operation where I work, and I operate it every day — so I live with the consequences of my own decisions.
